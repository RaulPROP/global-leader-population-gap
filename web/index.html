How much older are leaders than the people they lead?

For every country and every year since 1960: the age of the country's effective leader minus the mean (average) age of its population. A gap of +30 means the leader is 30 years older than the average resident.

About the data

What is compared

For each country and year, the leader's age on 1 July is compared with the mean (average) age of the population at the same date. The gap is leader age − mean age.

Population

Mean age is computed from the UN World Population Prospects 2024 population by single year of age: each one-year age group counts at its midpoint (e.g. 34.5), and the open-ended 100+ group at 102. Estimates run to ; later years use the UN medium-variant projection (dashed lines). Population totals (for the population-weighted average) come from the same source.

Who counts as "the leader"

The effective leader, i.e. the person who actually holds power, as coded by the REIGN dataset (monthly, 1950 – Aug 2021), which follows the Archigos definition. So: the US president, the UK prime minister, the Chinese party chief, a junta leader after a coup.

When several leaders held office in a year, the one who held it for the most days is used; the others are listed in the country panel.

After August 2021

REIGN stops in August 2021. After that, each country's series continues with Wikidata's holders of the same office REIGN was tracking (head of state or head of government), with a few hand-curated exceptions listed below.

Names and birth dates

Leaders are matched to Wikidata by country, dates in office and name, and take their English Wikipedia name and Wikidata birth date. Otherwise the birth date comes from Archigos or is inferred from REIGN's monthly age readings (shown as “c. 1950”).

Juntas and joint offices that REIGN codes as one leader (e.g. San Marino's two Captains Regent) have no single age, so no gap.

Historical states

Data is shown on today's borders. Before independence, former Soviet, Yugoslav and Czechoslovak republics show the leader of the union they belonged to. Germany before 1990 shows West Germany's leader, Yemen before 1990 North Yemen's, and Vietnam before 1976 North Vietnam's, each compared with the population of today's whole country. Colonies before independence have no leader.

Hand-curated decisions

    Caveats

    • "Effective leader" is a judgement call in collective or contested systems.
    • San Marino and Bosnia and Herzegovina have collective heads of state; they are not tracked after 2021.
    • Taiwan and Palestine are not covered by REIGN, so they have no data.
